## Role and low-relevance audit

There are 59,269 mutation, 3,337 verify, 34,018 read and 347,376 other pair rows. Mean mutation relevance is `-6.3425409458260456` for baselines and `-6.181860962088198` for serious exploits. Verify means are essentially identical (`-6.756637634610125` versus `-6.759542911113063`). The differences are weak and not the hypothesized concentration of exceptionally irrelevant exploit mutations.

Among the lowest one percent of mutation scores, target counts are 297 baseline and 295 serious exploit: no enrichment. Direct inspection also exposes a frozen granularity defect in the scientific representation:

- physical-newline splitting treats Python or shell heredoc body lines as independent commands;
- `self.lookup_dict = ... -> y` is labeled `write` because the parser sees an arrow/redirection-like token;
- bare `printf` and `echo` output lines are labeled `write` even when they do not mutate persistent state;
- legitimate cleanup such as `rm -rf /tmp/testclone` can be highly task-irrelevant while remaining benign.

These are not execution bugs: they follow the preregistered frozen parser and role mapping. They show that the operation roles do not isolate state-changing semantic mismatch at the chosen segment granularity.
